Best Peptides for Sleep & Stress Management: Evidence-Ranked Guide

Sleep and stress management is one of the newest categories in peptide research. It includes FDA-approved neuropeptides with established medical uses (vasopressin, oxytocin), Russian-developed anxiolytic peptides (Selank, Semax), and research compounds like DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide) that have been studied for decades with inconsistent results. This reference profiles 19 peptides and compounds studied for sleep regulation, stress response, and anxiety. Each is ranked by scientific evidence strength, with FDA status clearly marked.

How to Read Evidence Ratings

★★★★★
Strong Clinical Evidence
Multiple high-quality human clinical studies consistently support the effect.
★★★★☆
Good Human Evidence
Meaningful human data, but may be limited by study size or design.
★★★☆☆
Preliminary Human Evidence
Some human research exists, but early, limited, or mixed.
★★☆☆☆
Primarily Preclinical
Evidence from animal or lab studies. Limited or no human data.
★☆☆☆☆
Mechanistic Only
Plausible mechanism, but little direct evidence for the claimed outcome.
★★★★☆ / ★★☆☆☆
Split Rating
Different evidence by use (topical vs. injectable, approved vs. off-label).

Important: Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety.

Top Compounds

Selank (★★★☆☆), Approved in Russia

An anxiolytic (anti-anxiety) peptide with published human data on stress reduction. Approved in Russia; research-only in the US. Related to Semax but with a stronger anxiolytic profile.

Semax (★★★☆☆), Approved in Russia

Studied for both cognitive enhancement and stress response. Published human data, primarily Russian literature. May have stress-modulating effects alongside its cognitive profile.

Vasopressin / AVP (★★★☆☆/★★☆☆☆ split), FDA-Approved

FDA-approved for specific medical uses (diabetes insipidus, shock) at ★★★☆☆. Sleep-specific applications have weaker evidence (★★☆☆☆). An example of a peptide with strong approved-use evidence but limited sleep/stress evidence.

Epithalon (★★★☆☆), Research

Some human sleep and circadian rhythm data in Russian literature, alongside its longevity research. May affect melatonin production and sleep cycles.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is DSIP effective for sleep?
DSIP (Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide) has been studied since the 1970s. Human studies exist but results are inconsistent. Evidence rating: ★★☆☆☆ (primarily preclinical with mixed human results). It is not FDA-approved for sleep disorders.
What is Selank and does it help with anxiety?
Selank is a synthetic peptide approved in Russia for anxiety treatment. It works with brain opioid, serotonin, and GABA systems. Published human anxiolytic data exists, primarily in Russian literature. Evidence rating: ★★★☆☆. It is not FDA-approved in the US.
Can oxytocin help with stress?
Oxytocin has meaningful human data for stress and anxiety reduction (★★★☆☆). It is FDA-approved for obstetric use (Pitocin) but not specifically for stress or anxiety. Sleep-related claims have weaker evidence. It requires a prescription.
Are there FDA-approved peptides for sleep?
No peptide is FDA-approved specifically for sleep enhancement. Vasopressin is FDA-approved for diabetes insipidus and shock. Oxytocin is FDA-approved for obstetric use. Neither is approved for sleep disorders. All other peptides in this category are research compounds.
What is the best peptide for sleep?
No peptide has robust human clinical evidence specifically for sleep improvement. DSIP has been studied longest but with inconsistent results (★★☆☆☆). Epithalon has some circadian rhythm data (★★★☆☆). The evidence base for sleep peptides is one of the weakest across all categories.
Do peptides interact with sleep medications?
Potentially. Several peptides in this category interact with neurotransmitter systems (GABA, serotonin, opioid) that are also targeted by sleep medications. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before combining any peptide with sleep medications.
