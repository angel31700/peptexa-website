Best Peptides for Cognitive Enhancement: Evidence-Ranked Guide

Cognitive enhancement peptides represent a frontier area. The best-studied compounds, Cerebrolysin, Semax, and Selank, are approved in Russia, Europe, or Asia but remain research-only in the US. This category also includes nootropic peptides like Noopept, frontier compounds like Dihexa, and neurotrophic factor fragments that are still purely experimental. This reference profiles 22 peptides and compounds studied for cognitive enhancement, neuroprotection, and brain function. Each is ranked by scientific evidence strength, with international approval status clearly marked.

How to Read Evidence Ratings

★★★★★
Strong Clinical Evidence
Multiple high-quality human clinical studies consistently support the effect.
★★★★☆
Good Human Evidence
Meaningful human data, but may be limited by study size or design.
★★★☆☆
Preliminary Human Evidence
Some human research exists, but early, limited, or mixed.
★★☆☆☆
Primarily Preclinical
Evidence from animal or lab studies. Limited or no human data.
★☆☆☆☆
Mechanistic Only
Plausible mechanism, but little direct evidence for the claimed outcome.
★★★★☆ / ★★☆☆☆
Split Rating
Different evidence by use (topical vs. injectable, approved vs. off-label).

Important: Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety.

Top Compounds

Semax (★★★☆☆), Approved in Russia

A synthetic ACTH fragment studied for neuroprotection and cognitive enhancement. Published human studies exist, primarily in Russian literature. Approved in Russia; research-only in the US.

Selank (★★★☆☆), Approved in Russia

An anxiolytic peptide studied for stress reduction and cognitive function. Published human data on anxiety; cognitive enhancement evidence is more limited. Approved in Russia; research-only in the US.

SS-31 / Elamipretide (★★★☆☆), Clinical-Stage

Phase II human trials completed, primarily for mitochondrial disease. Cognitive applications are investigational. An interesting compound at the intersection of mitochondrial health and neurological function.

Thymosin Alpha-1 (★★★☆☆), Approved in 35+ countries

Primarily an immune modulator, but cognitive applications are emerging. The immune-brain connection is an active research area.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Semax and does it work?
Semax is a synthetic peptide derived from ACTH, studied for neuroprotection and cognitive enhancement. Russian human studies show positive effects on memory and attention. Evidence rating: ★★★☆☆ (preliminary human evidence, primarily Russian literature). It is approved in Russia but not FDA-approved in the US.
What is the best peptide for brain function?
No peptide is FDA-approved for cognitive enhancement in healthy individuals. Cerebrolysin has the most RCT data but for stroke/dementia, not healthy cognition (★★★☆☆). Semax and Selank have published human studies from Russia (★★★☆☆). All are rated ★★★☆☆ or lower for cognitive enhancement specifically.
Is Dihexa effective for cognition?
Dihexa is a synthetic peptide studied for cognitive enhancement and synaptogenesis. Evidence is primarily animal-based with no published human trials. Evidence rating: ★☆☆☆☆ (mechanistic/preliminary only). It is not approved for human use.
What is Cerebrolysin used for?
Cerebrolysin is approved in several European and Asian countries for stroke recovery and dementia. It has RCT data for these indications. Evidence for healthy cognitive enhancement is more limited. It is not FDA-approved in the US.
Are nootropic peptides safe?
Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety. Most cognitive enhancement peptides are research compounds or approved only abroad. No peptide is FDA-approved for cognitive enhancement in healthy people. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before considering any cognitive enhancement compound.
What is the difference between Semax and Selank?
Semax is derived from ACTH and studied primarily for cognitive enhancement and neuroprotection. Selank is an anxiolytic (anti-anxiety) peptide also studied for cognitive function. Both are approved in Russia, both are rated ★★★☆☆, and both are research-only in the US. They are sometimes used together in Russian medical practice.
