Best Peptides for Sexual Function & Libido: Evidence-Ranked Guide

Peptides for sexual function span from FDA-approved drugs with extensive clinical data to research compounds with anecdotal followings. This category includes hormones (GnRH analogs, oxytocin), melanocortin agents (PT-141), and emerging research peptides (kisspeptin). It also covers the most-searched peptide in this space: PT-141 (bremelanotide/Vyleesi), which is FDA-approved for female sexual desire dysfunction. This reference profiles 21 peptides and compounds studied for sexual function, libido, and reproductive health. Each is ranked by scientific evidence strength, with FDA status clearly marked.

How to Read Evidence Ratings

★★★★★
Strong Clinical Evidence
Multiple high-quality human clinical studies consistently support the effect.
★★★★☆
Good Human Evidence
Meaningful human data, but may be limited by study size or design.
★★★☆☆
Preliminary Human Evidence
Some human research exists, but early, limited, or mixed.
★★☆☆☆
Primarily Preclinical
Evidence from animal or lab studies. Limited or no human data.
★☆☆☆☆
Mechanistic Only
Plausible mechanism, but little direct evidence for the claimed outcome.
★★★★☆ / ★★☆☆☆
Split Rating
Different evidence by use (topical vs. injectable, approved vs. off-label).

Important: Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety.

Top Compounds

Gonadorelin (★★★★★), FDA-Approved

Synthetic GnRH, FDA-approved for fertility and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Strong clinical evidence for reproductive hormone function.

PGE1 / Alprostadil (★★★★★), FDA-Approved

FDA-approved for erectile dysfunction (Caverject injection, MUSE urethral suppository). One of the best-evidenced treatments for ED.

Oxytocin (★★★★☆), FDA-Approved (prescription)

FDA-approved for obstetric use (Pitocin). Evidence for sexual function and bonding is good but more limited than its obstetric use (★★★★☆ for sexual/bonding applications).

Kisspeptin-10 (★★★☆☆), Research

Multiple human studies on reproductive hormone signaling. Shows promise for testosterone modulation and fertility research, but not approved for sexual function use.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PT-141 and is it FDA-approved?
PT-141 (bremelanotide) is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD). It works through melanocortin receptors in the brain, unlike ED drugs which work through vascular mechanisms. Evidence rating: ★★★★☆. It is a prescription medication.
Does PT-141 work for men?
PT-141 is FDA-approved only for female HSDD. Some studies and anecdotal reports suggest it may affect male libido, but it is not FDA-approved for men. Consult a healthcare provider about off-label use.
What is the difference between PT-141 and Viagra?
Viagra (sildenafil) works through vascular mechanisms to improve blood flow for erectile function. PT-141 (bremelanotide) works through melanocortin receptors in the brain to affect sexual desire. PT-141 targets libido; Viagra targets the physical mechanism of erection.
What is Kisspeptin and does it boost testosterone?
Kisspeptin is a neuropeptide that stimulates GnRH release, which controls the reproductive hormone axis. Human studies show it can influence testosterone and LH levels. Evidence rating: ★★★☆☆ (preliminary human evidence). It is not FDA-approved.
Are there peptides for erectile dysfunction?
Yes. PGE1/alprostadil (Caverject, MUSE) is FDA-approved for ED (★★★★★). VIP (Invicorp) is approved in some countries for ED (★★★☆☆). Melanotan II has been studied for ED but is not approved and has safety concerns (★★☆☆☆).
Is Melanotan II safe for libido?
Melanotan II (MT-2) is a research compound (★★☆☆☆) with some human pilot studies for libido/ED effects. It is not FDA-approved and has documented side effects including skin darkening, nausea, and potential melanoma risk. It is not recommended for use without medical supervision.
