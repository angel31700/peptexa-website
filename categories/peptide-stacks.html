Peptide Stacks & Combinations: Evidence-Ranked Guide

Peptide stacking, combining two or more peptides to target multiple pathways at once, is one of the most popular and least-evidenced practices in the biohacking world. Named stacks like the GLOW Protocol, KLOW, the Wolverine Stack, and the CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin combo have massive followings, but most have never been tested in human combination trials. This reference profiles 15+ named peptide stacks, each rated by the combined evidence strength of its component compounds. We rate the individual peptides, and we tell you honestly when the combination itself has no clinical data. No dosing, no protocols, just the research, clearly rated. **The evidence gap:** Most peptide stacks are theoretical combinations. Individual compounds may have evidence, but the combination has usually never been studied in a human trial. We mark this clearly. A stack is not stronger than its weakest evidence link.

How to Read Evidence Ratings

★★★★★
Strong Clinical Evidence
Multiple high-quality human clinical studies consistently support the effect.
★★★★☆
Good Human Evidence
Meaningful human data, but may be limited by study size or design.
★★★☆☆
Preliminary Human Evidence
Some human research exists, but early, limited, or mixed.
★★☆☆☆
Primarily Preclinical
Evidence from animal or lab studies. Limited or no human data.
★☆☆☆☆
Mechanistic Only
Plausible mechanism, but little direct evidence for the claimed outcome.
★★★★☆ / ★★☆☆☆
Split Rating
Different evidence by use (topical vs. injectable, approved vs. off-label).

Important: Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GLOW peptide stack?
The GLOW protocol combines three peptides, GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500, typically in a 5:1:1 ratio. It was popularized by Jay Campbell for tissue repair, gut healing, and skin rejuvenation. Each compound has individual evidence (GHK-Cu topical ★★★★☆, BPC-157 ★★☆☆☆, TB-500 ★★☆☆☆), but no published human trial has tested them together. The combination is theoretical.
What is the KLOW peptide stack?
KLOW is the GLOW stack (GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500) with the addition of KPV, an anti-inflammatory tripeptide. It's designed to add an anti-inflammatory component to the healing and skin-renewal effects of GLOW. No combination trial data exists. KPV itself is rated ★★☆☆☆ (primarily preclinical).
What is the Wolverine peptide stack?
The Wolverine Stack combines BPC-157 and TB-500 for tissue repair and injury recovery. Ben Greenfield's full version adds ipamorelin, tesamorelin, and GHK-Cu. It's one of the most searched peptide stacks. No human combination trials exist; evidence is based on individual compound animal data and extensive anecdotal reports.
Can you stack BPC-157 with TB-500?
BPC-157 and TB-500 are the most commonly stacked peptide pair. BPC-157 is studied for local repair signaling (★★☆☆☆, primarily animal), and TB-500 (a Thymosin Beta-4 analog) for systemic recovery (★★☆☆☆, primarily animal). No published human trial has tested the combination. The pairing is theoretical, based on complementary mechanisms.
What is the best peptide stack for weight loss?
No peptide stack has human combination trial data for weight loss. The most evidence-backed individual peptides for weight loss are FDA-approved GLP-1 agonists (semaglutide ★★★★★, tirzepatide ★★★★★), which are typically used alone, not stacked. "Fat blaster" stacks combining MOTS-c and Sermorelin have weaker individual evidence (★★☆☆☆ and ★★★☆☆) and no combination data.
Is peptide stacking safe?
Safety data for peptide combinations is extremely limited. Each compound may have known side effects individually, but interactions between stacked compounds are largely unstudied. Star ratings measure evidence strength, NOT safety. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before considering any peptide stack.
What is the CJC-1295 and Ipamorelin stack?
CJC-1295 + Ipamorelin is the most popular growth hormone secretagogue stack, taken together at bedtime to stimulate GH release. CJC-1295 (a GHRH analog) and Ipamorelin (a GHRP) work through complementary pathways. Both are rated ★★☆☆☆ individually. No combination human trial data exists for the stack specifically.
How many peptides can you stack at once?
There is no clinically established safe maximum for stacked peptides. Named stacks range from 2 to 6 compounds. More compounds increase complexity and potential interactions without necessarily increasing efficacy. There is no evidence that larger stacks outperform smaller ones.
