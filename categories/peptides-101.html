Peptides 101: The Complete Beginner's Guide to Peptide Therapy

If you're new to peptides, this is your starting point. Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as biological messengers in the body, signaling cells to repair, regulate, and restore function. Some are naturally produced by your body; others are synthesized for research and therapeutic use. This guide covers the fundamentals: what peptides are, how they work, how to understand FDA status, how to read evidence ratings, and how to find the right category for your health goals. No dosing, no protocols, just the foundation you need to understand the peptide landscape.

How to Read Evidence Ratings

★★★★★
Strong Clinical Evidence
Multiple high-quality human clinical studies consistently support the effect.
★★★★☆
Good Human Evidence
Meaningful human data, but may be limited by study size or design.
★★★☆☆
Preliminary Human Evidence
Some human research exists, but early, limited, or mixed.
★★☆☆☆
Primarily Preclinical
Evidence from animal or lab studies. Limited or no human data.
★☆☆☆☆
Mechanistic Only
Plausible mechanism, but little direct evidence for the claimed outcome.

Split Rating
Different evidence by use (topical vs. injectable, approved vs. off-label).

Important: Star ratings measure evidence strength, not safety.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a peptide?
A peptide is a short chain of amino acids (typically 2-50) that acts as a biological messenger in the body. Peptides bind to specific cell receptors and trigger particular responses, from healing and repair to appetite regulation and immune function.
Are peptides safe?
Safety depends on the specific peptide, the individual, and the context of use. FDA-approved peptides have established safety profiles from clinical trials. Research peptides have limited or no human safety data. Peptexa's star ratings measure evidence strength, NOT safety. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider before considering any peptide.
Are peptides steroids?
No. Peptides and steroids are completely different classes of compounds. Peptides are amino acid chains that signal cells. Steroids are hormones that enter cells and directly alter gene expression. Some peptides affect hormone systems (like growth hormone), but they are not steroids.
Do I need a prescription for peptides?
FDA-approved peptides (semaglutide, tirzepatide, PT-141, etc.) require a prescription. Compounded peptides require a prescription from a physician. Research peptides are sold by research chemical suppliers without a prescription but are labeled "not for human use." Dietary supplement and cosmetic peptides are available over the counter.
How do I know which peptide is right for me?
Start with your health goal. Browse the category that matches what you're looking for (weight loss, recovery, anti-aging, etc.) and compare evidence ratings. Take the [Peptide Quiz](/peptide-quiz.html) for a personalized recommendation. Then discuss your findings with a qualified healthcare provider.
What does "research peptide" mean?
"Research peptide" means the compound is not FDA-approved for human use. It may be studied in laboratory or animal research, or in early clinical trials. The label does not indicate quality, it indicates regulatory status. Always consult a healthcare provider before considering any research compound.
