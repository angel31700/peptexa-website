KPV, what the science actually shows

KPV is a tripeptide fragment of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH) studied for anti-inflammatory effects. Not FDA-approved. Evidence base: primarily preclinical anti-inflammatory data; FDA concluded available data are not well-characterized despite zero FAERS adverse-event reports.

A naturally occurring tripeptide from alpha-MSH. Retains anti-inflammatory activity without melanocortin binding (no skin darkening). Researched for IBD, wound healing, and as part of the trending KLOW stack.

What KPV is

KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) consists of the last three amino acids of alpha-MSH (positions 11-13). Alpha-MSH has both pigmentation and anti-inflammatory effects. KPV retains potent anti-inflammatory activity while lacking melanocortin receptor affinity, no skin tanning side effect.

Mechanism, in plain English

Enters cells and inhibits NF-kB inflammatory signaling by interacting with the IKK complex. This prevents degradation of IkappaB, keeping NF-kB suppressed. Reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha) and inflammatory enzymes (iNOS, COX-2). Works through a melanocortin receptor-independent pathway.

What the evidence shows

Animal studies: reduces colitis inflammation (IBD models), accelerates wound healing topically. In vitro: inhibits inflammatory cytokines in intestinal epithelial cells, macrophages, microglia. Oral nanoparticle delivery reduced colitis severity comparably to steroids in mice. No published human clinical trials. Used in biohacking's 'KLOW stack.'

FDA status

Not FDA-approved. No human clinical trials registered. Research-stage compound.

Common synonyms

KPV, Lys-Pro-Val, alpha-MSH(11-13)

Safety and adverse-event landscape

No human safety data from trials. Animal studies show good tolerability. Naturally occurring alpha-MSH fragment. Unregulated products have unknown purity.
